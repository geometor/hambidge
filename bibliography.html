Bibliography¶

Primary Yale University Press Editions, Classical Treatises & Botanical Sources

Jay Hambidge — The Diagonal: An Illustrated Monthly Magazine Devoted to the Explanation of the Rediscovered Principles of Symmetry and Proportion in Nature and to the Application of Them to Modern Art and Design

Vol. I, Nos. 1–12 (Nov. 1919 – Oct. 1920), 232 pp., New Haven: Yale University Press, 1920.

Primary public-domain serial source of Hambidge’s Elements of Dynamic Symmetry lessons: sunflower phyllotaxis, root-rectangles ($\sqrt{1}\text{–}\sqrt{5}$), reciprocal diagonals, and whirling squares.

Jay Hambidge — Dynamic Symmetry: The Greek Vase

xvi + 161 pp., 16 plates, New Haven: Yale University Press, 1920.

Hambidge’s foundational monograph demonstrating dynamic symmetry in classical Greek pottery, kylikes, and amphorae via root-five and whirling-square area dissection.

Arthur Harry Church — On the Relation of Phyllotaxis to Mechanical Laws

Parts I–III, 353 pp., 112 plates, London & Oxford: Williams & Norgate, 1904.

Exhaustive botanical monograph with precision logarithmic spiral diagrams of sunflower capitula (Helianthus annuus) providing empirical data for both Thompson and Hambidge.

Euclid of Alexandria — The Thirteen Books of Euclid’s Elements

3 vols., translated from the text of Heiberg with introduction and commentary by Sir Thomas L. Heath (2nd ed. rev.), Cambridge: at the University Press, 1926.

Axiomatic source of Book I Prop. 47 (Pythagoras), Book II Def. 2 (gnomon), Book II Prop. 11 (golden cut), Book VI Prop. 30 (extreme and mean ratio), Book X Def. 2 (dynamei symmetroi), and Book XIII (regular polyhedra).

Johannes Kepler — Strena seu de Nive Sexangula (A New Year’s Gift of Hexagonal Snow)

24 pp., Frankfurt am Main: Gottfried Tampach, 1611.

Celebrated treatise linking hexagonal snow crystallization, bee’s cell close-packing, the rhombic dodecahedron, and the golden ratio as a “measure of gold.”

Sir D’Arcy Wentworth Thompson — On Growth and Form

xv + 793 pp., 408 figs., Cambridge: at the University Press, 1917.

Landmark synthesis of mathematical morphology, surface tension, logarithmic spirals, Hero’s gnomon, and coordinate transformations.
