Botanical Close-Packing Across phyllotaxis, divine, and thompson¶

  • Church, Tait & Thompson (`geometor/thompson` & `geometor/phyllotaxis`): Both Sir D'Arcy Thompson (Chapter XIV) and Hambidge take A. H. Church's Oxford sunflower plates as their benchmark. Where Thompson focuses on mechanical close-packing and P. G. Tait’s cylindrical parastichy lattice, Hambidge extracts the synthetic area geometry: the ratio of consecutive seed counts approaches the cascade multiplier $\phi$, linking discoidal phyllotaxis to the $\sqrt{5}$ rectangle.

  • Why the Golden Ratio Governs Seed Packing (`geometor/divine`): In continued fraction theory, $\phi = [1; 1, 1, \dots]$ possesses the slowest-converging expansion, avoiding premature radial alignment and enabling uniform density across all packing radii.
