Botanical Close-Packing Across `phyllotaxis`, `divine`, and `thompson`
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

* **Church, Tait & Thompson (`geometor/thompson` & `geometor/phyllotaxis`)**: Both :person:`Sir D'Arcy Thompson <thompson>` (Chapter XIV) and Hambidge take :person:`A. H. Church's <church>` Oxford sunflower plates as their benchmark. Where Thompson focuses on mechanical close-packing and P. G. Tait's cylindrical parastichy lattice, Hambidge extracts the synthetic area geometry: the ratio of consecutive seed counts approaches the cascade multiplier :math:`\phi`, linking discoidal phyllotaxis to the :math:`\sqrt{5}` rectangle.
* **Why the Golden Ratio Governs Seed Packing (`geometor/divine`)**: In continued fraction theory, :math:`\phi = [1; 1, 1, \dots]` possesses the slowest-converging expansion, avoiding premature radial alignment and enabling uniform density across all packing radii.
