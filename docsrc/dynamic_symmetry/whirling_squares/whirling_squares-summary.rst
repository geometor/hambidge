The Root-Five Rectangle & The Whirling Squares — Summary
========================================================

.. rubric:: Analytical Synopsis — *The Elements of Dynamic Symmetry*, Lesson I (*The Diagonal*, 1919, pp. 15–17)

Overview of Section III
-----------------------

Lesson I develops the primary dynamic harmonic shape derived from plant phyllotaxis—the $\sqrt{5}$ rectangle—and its constituent Golden Rectangle:

1. **The Root-Five Rectangle ($1 : \sqrt{5}$)**: The square on the end is exactly one-fifth of the square on the side. Constructed by bisecting the unit square and swinging the half-diagonal $\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}$ to both sides of the base.
2. **Decomposition into Golden Rectangles**: The $\sqrt{5}$ rectangle consists of a central unit square flanked symmetrically by two reciprocal golden rectangles ($\phi^{-1} \approx 0.618034$): $\sqrt{5} = 1 + 2\phi^{-1}$.
3. **The Rectangle of the Whirling Squares ($1 : \phi$)**: Constructed by swinging the half-diagonal to one side ($1 : \frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}$).
4. **Reciprocals & Gnomonic Whirling Squares**: The reciprocal rectangle of ratio $1/\phi = \phi - 1$ cuts the parent diagonal at $90^\circ$. Successive detachment of square gnomons forms a spiral whirling toward the pole of perpendicular diagonals.
5. **Two Golden Rectangles Overlapping**: Two overlapping $1 : \phi$ rectangles sharing a central unit square reconstitute the parent $\sqrt{5}$ rectangle: $2\phi - 1 = \sqrt{5}$.
