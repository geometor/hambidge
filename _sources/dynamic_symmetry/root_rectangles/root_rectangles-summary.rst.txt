Root Rectangles and Commensurability in Square — Summary
========================================================

.. rubric:: Analytical Synopsis — *The Elements of Dynamic Symmetry*, Lesson II (*The Diagonal*, 1919, pp. 18–21)

Overview of Section IV
----------------------

Lesson II develops the Euclidean and operational principles of the root-rectangle family ($\sqrt{1}, \sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3}, \sqrt{4}, \sqrt{5}$):

1. **Commensurability in Square (`Euclid Book X, Def. 2`)**: The fundamental Greek concept of *dynamei symmetroi*—lengths irrational in linear measure whose squares have exact integer ratios ($1 : 2 : 3 : 4 : 5$).
2. **Pythagorean Diagonal Compass Unfolding**: Swinging each successive diagonal down to the baseline increments the side quadrance by exact integer steps ($Q = 1 \to 2 \to 3 \to 4 \to 5$), as praised by :person:`Kepler <kepler>` as "a measure of gold."
3. **Reciprocal Rectangles ($1 / \sqrt{n}$)**: A reciprocal rectangle divides the parent $\sqrt{n}$ rectangle into $n$ congruent similar parts.
4. **Reciprocal Orthogonality**: The diagonal of any reciprocal rectangle meets the main diagonal of the parent rectangle at an exact $90^\circ$ right angle (perpendicular spread $s = 1$).
5. **Three Classical Geometric Constructions**: Demonstrates reciprocal extraction via: (1) unit square intersection with main diagonal; (2) perpendicular erection at a vertex; (3) Thales's semicircle on the end (Euclid III.31).
