Static and Dynamic Symmetry in Nature and Greek Art — Summary
=============================================================

.. rubric:: Analytical Synopsis — *Dynamic Symmetry: The Greek Vase* (1920) & *The Diagonal* (1919–1920)

Overview of Section I
---------------------

In the opening texts of *Dynamic Symmetry: The Greek Vase* (:cite:`hambidge:1920-vase`) and *The Diagonal* (:cite:`hambidge:1920-diagonal`), :person:`Jay Hambidge <hambidge>` articulates the core dichotomy of proportional design:

1. **Static Symmetry**: Passive, fixed-unit proportion grounded in regular polygons (squares, equilateral triangles, hexagons) and linear modular measurement. It appears in mineral crystals, diatoms, radiolaria, and Roman, Byzantine, Gothic, and modern decorative art.
2. **Dynamic Symmetry**: Active, organic proportion grounded in logarithmic shell growth, leaf-distribution (phyllotaxis), and Euclidean area commensurability. Known historically only to Egypt and classical Greece.
3. **The Egyptian Harpedonaptae**: Traces the empirical origins of survey geometry to the 3–4–5 right-angled rope-stretching (*cording of the temple*), transmitted to Greece in the 6th–7th centuries BCE and perfected as practical and Euclidean geometry (:cite:`euclid:elements`).
