Types / person

Content of type 'person'

Church

Author of On the Relation of Phyllotaxis to Mechanical Laws (church:1904), whose precision diagrams of intersecting orthogonal logarithmic spirals in sunflower capitula (\(34/55\), \(55/89\), \(89/144\)) directly inspired both D'Arcy Thompson's morphology and Lesson I of Jay Hambidge’s Dynamic Symmetry.

Euclid

Provides the axiomatic bedrock of Dynamic Symmetry: Book I Prop. 47 (Pythagoras’s Theorem), Book II Def. 2 (gnomon), Book II Prop. 11 & Book VI Prop. 30 (extreme and mean ratio \(\phi\)), and especially Book X Def. 2 (magnitudes commensurable in square, dynamei symmetroi)—the literal Greek source of Hambidge’s ‘dynamic’ proportion.

Hambidge

Canadian-American artist and geometric scholar who rediscovered the Greek canon of proportional areas based on Euclidean commensurability in square (euclid:elements, Book X Def. 2), publishing The Diagonal (1919–1920) and Dynamic Symmetry: The Greek Vase (1920) at Yale University Press.

Kepler

Celebrated the golden ratio alongside Pythagoras’s Theorem as the two great treasures of geometry in Strena seu de Nive Sexangula (kepler:1611), quoted by Hambidge in Lesson II of The Elements of Dynamic Symmetry.

Phiarchitect

Creator of the GEOMETOR Digital Scriptorium editions, exact symbolic geometry models, and semantic cross-repository apparatus.

Plato

In the Theaetetus (147d–148b), records Theodorus of Cyrene’s demonstration of the linear incommensurability of roots from \(\sqrt{3}\) to \(\sqrt{17}\) and introduces the concept of dynamis (square power) from which commensurability in square derives.

Thompson

Author of On Growth and Form (thompson:1917), whose analysis of equiangular spirals, gnomonic growth in molluscan shells, and botanical phyllotaxis directly complements Hambidge’s synthetic dynamic symmetry and root rectangles.
